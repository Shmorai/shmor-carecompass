HSA changes for 2026 and 2027: what is new

The 2025 federal budget law expanded Health Savings Accounts from 2026. More people can now open one, and they can be used in new ways. Here is a summary, plus the 2027 contribution limits.

By the CareCompass editorial team · 3 October 2026 · Updated 15 October 2026 · 1 min read

Photo: Windows on Unsplash

What changed in 2026

  • Bronze and catastrophic plans: individual bronze and catastrophic plans available through an exchange are treated as HSA-qualifying high-deductible plans.
  • Direct primary care: people in qualifying DPC arrangements can contribute to an HSA, and can use HSA funds to pay the membership fees, within a monthly limit (the IRS set it at $150 for an individual and $300 for a family arrangement).
  • Telehealth: plans can cover telehealth before the deductible without affecting HSA eligibility, now on a permanent basis.

2027 limits

For 2027 the IRS has set HSA contribution limits of $4,500 for self-only coverage and $9,000 for family coverage, with an extra $1,000 catch-up contribution for people aged 55 or older.

Using your HSA for dental and cosmetic care

HSA funds can generally pay for qualified dental care such as cleanings, fillings, braces and implants. Purely cosmetic procedures, such as teeth whitening or elective cosmetic surgery, generally do not qualify. See dental care and direct primary care.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use my HSA for Invisalign?
Orthodontic treatment to correct teeth alignment is generally a qualified medical expense. Keep receipts.
