Med spa safety: choosing who injects your Botox and fillers

Med spas are everywhere, and injectables are medical treatments. Here is how to choose a provider you can trust.

By the CareCompass editorial team · 4 October 2026 · Updated 15 October 2026 · 1 min read

Photo: karelys Ruiz on Unsplash

Who is allowed to inject

Rules vary by state. Depending on where you live, injections may be given by physicians, physician assistants, nurse practitioners or registered nurses, usually under some level of physician supervision. Ask who your injector is, their license type, and who the supervising doctor is. You can check licenses with your state licensing boards.

Watch for counterfeit products

The FDA and CDC have warned about counterfeit or unapproved botulinum toxin given by unlicensed people or in non-medical settings, with reports of serious harm. Ask to see the product box and confirm it is an FDA-approved brand obtained from a licensed source.

Questions to ask

  1. Who will inject me, and what is their training?
  2. Which product is being used, and is it FDA-approved?
  3. How are complications handled, and is there a doctor available?
  4. What is the total price, per unit or per syringe?

Frequently asked questions

What are signs of a problem after Botox?
Seek urgent medical care for difficulty swallowing, breathing or speaking, drooping eyelids, blurred vision or muscle weakness after injection.
