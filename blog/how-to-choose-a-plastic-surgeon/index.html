How to choose a plastic surgeon: a safety checklist

The surgeon you choose matters more than any discount. Use this checklist before you book cosmetic surgery.

By the CareCompass editorial team · 7 October 2026 · Updated 15 October 2026 · 1 min read

Photo: Piron Guillaume on Unsplash

Check credentials

  • Board certification: look for certification by the American Board of Plastic Surgery, or another board recognized by the American Board of Medical Specialties that is relevant to the procedure. Verify at CertificationMatters.org.
  • State medical license: check with your state medical board, including any disciplinary actions.
  • Hospital privileges: ask whether the surgeon is allowed to perform the same procedure at a local hospital.

Check the facility

Many cosmetic procedures take place in office-based surgical suites or ambulatory surgery centers. Ask whether the facility is accredited (for example by AAAASF, AAAHC or The Joint Commission), who provides anesthesia, and what happens in an emergency.

Red flags

  • Pressure to book on the day, or “today only” discounts.
  • No before-and-after photos of the surgeon’s own patients.
  • Unclear or unitemized pricing.
  • Multiple major procedures in one session without a clear safety explanation.
  • Reluctance to discuss risks or complications.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between a plastic surgeon and a cosmetic surgeon?
“Plastic surgeon” usually refers to a surgeon certified by the American Board of Plastic Surgery. “Cosmetic surgeon” can be used by doctors with different training, so always check the certifying board.
