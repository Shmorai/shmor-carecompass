Medical debt and your credit report: where things stand

A federal rule that would have removed medical debt from credit reports never took effect: a federal court vacated it in July 2025. Here is what still protects you and what to do about a medical bill you cannot pay.

By the CareCompass editorial team · 30 September 2026 · Updated 15 October 2026 · 1 min read

Photo: Anastasiia Gudantova on Unsplash

What happened to the federal rule

In January 2025 the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau (CFPB) finalized a rule to stop most medical debt from appearing on credit reports. In July 2025 a federal court in Texas ruled the CFPB had exceeded its authority and vacated the rule.

Protections that still apply

  • The three nationwide credit bureaus have voluntary policies that keep paid medical collections and smaller medical collection debts off reports, and wait before reporting new medical collections.
  • A number of states have their own laws limiting medical debt reporting.
  • You can dispute any inaccurate information with the credit bureau and the company that reported it.

Dealing with a medical bill

  1. Ask for an itemized bill and check it for errors.
  2. Check whether the No Surprises Act applies.
  3. Ask the hospital about financial assistance (charity care); nonprofit hospitals must have a policy.
  4. Negotiate a discount or an interest-free payment plan.
  5. Be cautious about putting medical bills on high-interest credit cards.

Frequently asked questions

Is medical debt still on credit reports?
It can be. The CFPB rule was vacated, but bureau policies and some state laws keep many medical debts off reports.
