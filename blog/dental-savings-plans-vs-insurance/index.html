Dental savings plans vs dental insurance

If you do not get dental insurance through work, you have two main choices: buy a dental insurance plan or join a dental savings plan. Here is how they compare.

By the CareCompass editorial team · 26 September 2026 · Updated 15 October 2026 · 1 min read

Photo: Caroline LM on Unsplash

Side by side

Dental insuranceDental savings plan
Is it insurance?YesNo: a discount membership
Annual maximumUsually yesUsually none
Waiting periodsCommon for basic and major careUsually none
Pre-existing conditionsMay be excludedUsually accepted
NetworkPPO or DHMO networkParticipating dentists only

In-house membership plans

Many dental practices now offer their own membership plans: a yearly or monthly fee that covers preventive visits plus a discount on other treatment. They can be good value if you are uninsured and like your dentist, but only work at that practice.

Which suits you?

  • Insurance may suit people who mainly want preventive care and can plan major work around waiting periods.
  • Savings plans may suit people who need treatment soon, have reached their annual maximum, or are on Medicare without dental coverage.

Before joining a discount plan, confirm your dentist participates. Check plan terms with your state insurance department if anything is unclear.

Frequently asked questions

Are dental savings plans legitimate?
Many are, but they are not insurance. Check which dentists participate and read the terms, including cancellation rules.
