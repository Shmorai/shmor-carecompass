Cosmetic dentistry

Teeth whitening and veneers: a brighter smile, safely

From in-office whitening to porcelain veneers, here is how cosmetic dentistry options compare and what to ask before you choose.

Photo: Kamal Hoseinianzade on Unsplash

Your whitening options

OptionHow it worksGood to know
In-office whiteningHigher-strength gel applied by a dental professionalFastest results; gums are protected
Custom take-home traysDentist-made trays with lower-strength gelGradual results; trays can be reused for touch-ups
Over-the-counter stripsPre-loaded strips worn dailyLower cost; less even results

Whitening does not change the color of crowns, veneers or fillings. Sensitivity is a common, usually temporary, side effect.

Veneers and bonding

Veneers are thin shells of porcelain or composite bonded to the front of teeth to change their color, shape or size. Porcelain veneers usually require removing some enamel, so the decision is generally irreversible. Composite bonding is a less invasive, lower-cost alternative that may need more upkeep.

Insurance and payment

Cosmetic dentistry is rarely covered by insurance and whitening is generally not HSA/FSA eligible. Ask for a free cosmetic consultation and a written estimate. See free consultations.

Last updated: 15 October 2026

Frequently asked questions

Is teeth whitening safe?
Whitening under the supervision of a dentist is generally considered safe. Have a check-up first, because cavities or gum problems should be treated before whitening.
Are veneers covered by insurance?
Usually not when they are purely cosmetic. If a tooth is damaged, a crown may be covered instead.
