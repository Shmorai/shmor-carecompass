Dental care

Dental care: finding a dentist you trust

From routine check-ups to crowns, here is how to find a dentist who takes your plan, what common treatments involve and how to keep costs predictable.

Photo: Caroline LM on Unsplash

🪥

Preventive care

Exams, cleanings and X-rays. Often covered at or near 100% by dental plans.

🦷

Restorative care

Fillings, root canals, crowns and bridges to repair damaged teeth.

😁

Cosmetic dentistry

Whitening, veneers and bonding. Usually not covered by insurance.

🚑

Emergency care

Severe pain, swelling or a knocked-out tooth needs same-day care.

How to choose a dentist

  • Check they are in-network with your exact plan (see our insurance guide).
  • Confirm their license with your state dental board.
  • Look for clear pricing, a written treatment plan and options to phase treatment.
  • Ask about new-patient specials and free consultations for bigger treatment.
  • For children, consider a pediatric dentist; see kids’ dental care and CHIP.

Common treatments, in plain English

TreatmentWhat it isTypical insurance category

Categories differ between plans. Check your summary of benefits.

Keeping costs down

  • Use your preventive benefits: twice-yearly check-ups catch problems early.
  • Ask for a pre-treatment estimate for anything major.
  • Time treatment around your plan’s annual maximum, which often resets each January.
  • Use HSA/FSA funds for eligible care (see HSA changes).
  • No insurance? Compare dental savings plans and low-cost options.

Frequently asked questions

How often should I see a dentist?
Many people are advised to have a check-up every six months, but your dentist may recommend a different interval based on your oral health.
How do I find a dentist that takes Medicaid?
Use your state Medicaid agency’s provider search, or InsureKidsNow.gov for children’s dental care, then call the practice to confirm they are accepting new Medicaid patients.
