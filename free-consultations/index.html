Free consultations: how to find one and get the most from it

A free consultation lets you meet a provider, understand your options and get a written plan before spending a cent. Here is how they work, and how to avoid surprise charges.

What a free consultation usually includes

A free consultation is typically a first meeting with the provider or a treatment coordinator. You can expect:

  • a conversation about your goals, health history and medications;
  • a visual examination or assessment;
  • an explanation of the treatment options, risks and recovery;
  • a written treatment plan with an estimated price and payment options.

What it usually does not include: diagnostic imaging (X-rays, CBCT 3D scans), lab work or a full clinical exam. These are often billed separately, and may or may not be covered by your insurance.

Where to find free consultations

  • Orthodontists for braces and clear aligners commonly offer a free first visit.
  • Implant and cosmetic dentists for implants, veneers and whitening.
  • Plastic surgeons for cosmetic surgery. Check board certification first.
  • Med spas for Botox and fillers.
  • Direct primary care and concierge practices (see private healthcare).

Look for “free consultation” on the practice website, or simply call and ask. Many practices offer virtual consultations too.

Questions to ask before you book

  1. Is the consultation completely free, or is there a fee that is credited towards treatment?
  2. Are X-rays or scans included? If not, what do they cost?
  3. Who will I see: the dentist or surgeon, or a coordinator?
  4. Are you in-network with my plan? (Ask for the plan name, not just the insurer.)
  5. Will I receive a written treatment plan and estimate?

Your right to a Good Faith Estimate

If you are uninsured or paying without insurance, federal rules under the No Surprises Act generally entitle you to a Good Faith Estimate of expected charges for scheduled care. If the final bill is substantially higher than the estimate, you may be able to dispute it. See our guide to surprise billing protections and the official information at CMS.gov/nosurprises.

Frequently asked questions

Why do practices offer free consultations?
For elective and cosmetic treatment, a free first visit lets patients compare providers without risk. It is a normal and legitimate practice, but you should never feel pressured to book treatment on the day.
Is a free consultation the same as a check-up?
No. A consultation discusses a specific treatment. A dental check-up or medical exam is a clinical service that is usually billed, though preventive care is often covered by insurance.
Can I get a second opinion?
Yes, and for major treatment such as implants or surgery it is a good idea. Free consultations make second opinions easier.
